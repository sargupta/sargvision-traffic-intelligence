---

## Traffic Data — what we have

Siliguri already has multiple sources of mobility information: historical travel
observations, maps, GPS-derived movement, and in future authorised city data.

**Raw data alone does not tell an officer what matters.**

## Traffic Monitoring — what is happening

Existing tools show what traffic looks like at a moment. They answer *"where is traffic
slow right now?"*

**Monitoring is reactive. It does not say whether a situation is normal, recurring or
unusual** — and that is the question an officer actually has.

## Traffic Intelligence — what it means

SARGVISION adds the intelligence layer:

- How does Siliguri move?
- What patterns are normal?
- Which journeys are unreliable?
- What is unusual today?
- **What deserves attention?**

## The pitch

> **Traffic Data tells us what we have.**
> **Traffic Monitoring tells us what is happening.**
> **Traffic Intelligence helps us understand what it means.**

## The sharper version, for investors and government

> Siliguri does not need another traffic dashboard.
>
> Traffic data already exists. Traffic monitoring tools already exist.
>
> **What is missing is an intelligence layer** that can transform mobility observations
> into an understanding of how the city moves, identify recurring and unusual patterns,
> and help authorities focus attention where it matters.
>
> **That is the gap SARGVISION solves.**

---

## Why this differentiates us from Google Maps

**Google provides navigation and traffic visibility. SARGVISION builds the intelligence
and decision-support layer around urban mobility.**

That is a complement, not a competition — and it is the honest description of both.

## The ladder is not just a slide — it is what we built

The framing holds because the engineering follows it exactly:

| Tier | In this product | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Data** | 101,418 valid primary-route observations | [provenance](data-provenance.md) |
| **Monitoring** | **Deliberately skipped** | Monitoring without intelligence is what already exists. We did not build another one |
| **Intelligence** | Baselines · anomaly detection · confidence · operational priority · evidence coverage | [spike](methodology/spatial-feasibility-spike.md) · [limitations](known-limitations.md) |
| **Decisions** | Prioritised attention, with what we do not know stated alongside | [design](design/ui-ux.md) |

> **We went straight from data to intelligence and skipped the monitoring tier on
> purpose.** A third traffic dashboard would have added nothing to Siliguri.

## What keeps the claim honest

The intelligence tier is only worth anything if it is trustworthy, so the product states
its own limits as prominently as its findings:

- every figure carries **confidence** and **sample size**;
- every figure records **which baseline level produced it**;
- the map shows **where we have no evidence** rather than rendering absence as calm;
- **42.4%** of observations are scored — we publish that, rather than implying city-wide coverage;
- the demonstrator says **historical replay**, never *live*.

**An intelligence layer that overclaims is just a dashboard with better adjectives.**
